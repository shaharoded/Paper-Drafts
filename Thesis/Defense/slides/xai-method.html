About 1 minute. Encoder explanations use sparse transcoders (Dunefsky et al., 2024), trained on the encoder's internal representations. They attach through a gradient-decoupled hook whose injected term is numerically zero, so the forward pass is bit-exact and the logits we explain are the deployed model's own logits, not those of a surrogate. Attributions are aggregated per token over 300 test patients. Because the vocabulary consists of named concepts, the result reads as "severe hypoalbuminemia pushes death risk up", not "feature 4,217". Limits to state if asked: these describe the model's local sensitivity, not causal necessity, and they describe the fitted function rather than the patient. Attribution stability across random initializations was not tested (single-seed training). The decoder needs no attribution machinery for its primary explanation: it emits the abstraction trajectory itself alongside a per-step risk curve.
