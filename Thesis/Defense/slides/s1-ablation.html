About 2 minutes. This is an important and slightly counter-intuitive result; say it plainly. Both ablations hold the deployed encoder fixed and vary only the input (AUPRC, support-weighted). - Replace the KBTA vocabulary with a standard-deviation (σ-bin) discretization, a close relative of SAX: AUPRC 0.676 vs 0.672, identical AUROC. Only cost: length-of-stay error up 3.2 h. - Collapse every interval to a one-second point event: AUPRC falls to 0.593, length-of-stay error up 7.4 h. So the architecture depends on persistence (a symbol holds over a span, and spans overlap) and not on the symbols being clinically named. On MIMIC-IV, KBTA is an interpretability substrate rather than a predictive prerequisite. This is negative for one claim and positive for another. It rules out claiming that clinical knowledge buys accuracy on this cohort. It establishes that interpretability comes at essentially no accuracy cost, which is the more useful claim, since interpretability bought with performance is rarely adopted. A σ-bin model predicts as well, but it cannot produce the explanations on the next slide, because its features have no names. Foreshadow: on Kineret, where the knowledge base was authored, the same substitution does cost accuracy (Study 3). The two results bound each other.
