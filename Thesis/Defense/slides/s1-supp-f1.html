- Best-F1: same story as AUPRC: Enc best on 4 of 6, severe hypoglycemia is the exception - Encoder: one direct probability per outcome, already well calibrated (fitted temperatures 0.87 to 1.04, ECE 0.016 → 0.013). Scaling is optional - Decoder problem: it outputs a risk at every generated step, and the patient's probability is the maximum over the whole trajectory. A peak over many steps is not a calibrated probability: the more steps, the more chances for a high value - How it shows up by prevalence: for common outcomes the peaks run too high (death, hyperglycemia: over-confident); for the rare hypoglycemia outcomes they run too low (under-confident). One readout, miscalibrated in opposite directions - What scaling does: one number per outcome, a temperature T, fitted on the validation set: p = σ(logit / T), then applied unchanged to test. T > 1 softens over-confident outputs, T < 1 sharpens under-confident ones. No retraining - What it fixed: death T = 1.47, ECE 0.050 → 0.011; hyperglycemia T = 1.24, 0.039 → 0.018. Rare hypoglycemia (T 0.65 to 0.78) improves only partly (severe 0.112 → 0.075) and stays the worst, too few positives - What it doesn't change: the ranking. Scaling is monotonic, so AUROC, AUPRC and best-F1 are identical before and after
