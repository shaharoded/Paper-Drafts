About 1.5 minutes. These fourteen are the exposure variable of the whole clinical study. Group them; do not read all of them. Monitoring: admission glucose monitoring (≥3 per 24 h for the first 3 days with documented diabetes, 2 otherwise); routine monitoring throughout the stay (≥3 per window while hyper- or hypoglycemic, ≥1 otherwise); monitoring in the first 2 days of corticosteroids. Admission medication management: BMI or weight within 48 h (decays to 0 at 72 h, 6-month carry-forward); creatinine within 48 h; stop high-hypoglycemia-risk antidiabetics in the first 72 h; stop SGLT2 inhibitors; continue home basal insulin within 36 h. Insulin administration and titration: reactive bolus or IV insulin overlapping a meal while glucose is elevated; withhold antidiabetics after hypoglycemia; reduce basal dose after hypoglycemia (a withheld basal dose counts as compliant); reduce bolus after hypoglycemia (scored only if a bolus was actually given); routine basal frequency 1 to 4 per window during hyperglycemia; routine basal dose 0.2 to 0.6 U/kg for insulin-naive patients (default weight 81 kg; home doses accepted as given). Note: BMI and creatinine on admission are two separate recommendations, as are the two stop-on-admission ones, which is why the Time column shows five lines for seven items. Why the asymmetry between basal and bolus: basal is routine, so no dose after hypoglycemia is a withheld dose; bolus is reactive, so its absence says nothing.
