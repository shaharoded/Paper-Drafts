About 1 minute. Center-level before vs after (FDR-adjusted): - Galilee Medical Center (GMC): 0.548 to 0.667, P < .001, the one clear improvement. - Barzilai (0.738 to 0.739) and Bnai Zion (0.727 to 0.731): no measurable change. - Hillel Yaffe (0.663 to 0.651, P < .001), Poria (0.766 to 0.753, P = .014) and Shamir (0.715 to 0.708, P = .047): small but significant declines. So the pooled increase is not reproduced uniformly, and the national figure alone would misrepresent every individual site, including those where practice did not move and those where it moved the wrong way. Why it matters: a national mean is the natural headline but the wrong unit to act on. An effort that worked in one hospital and not others calls for finding out what differed between them, not for repeating the same intervention nationally. Stratification is what makes that question askable. These data do not explain the heterogeneity.
