About 1.5 minutes. 1. A single-schema knowledge base for inpatient diabetes care. 174 TAK units encode the clinical semantics of inpatient care and the fourteen recommendations of the Israeli national position paper as QA patterns. Declarative and QA knowledge live in one representation, executed by one engine in one pass. Because it names clinical concepts rather than dataset columns, it transfers to any EHR in the same clinical scope once a concept index is supplied. 2. An automated, component-level measure of guideline adherence. Compliance becomes a continuous quantity per recommendation and per admission, comparable across hospitals and over time without chart review. At national scale across six hospitals it shows that adherence differs by the kind of action required, not by clinical weight. 3. Evidence on which recommendations matter. Under propensity weighting, above-median adherence carries substantially lower odds of 30-day mortality and hyperglycemia against a small rise in mild hypoglycemia. The least-adhered-to recommendations are among those most strongly associated with outcomes: the most actionable finding, and a direct target for decision support. 4. Guideline adherence as an explanatory variable. Procedural quality-of-care knowledge brought to bear on outcomes alongside the declarative knowledge such analyses normally rely on, both statistically as an exposure and as model input.
