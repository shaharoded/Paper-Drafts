About 1.5 minutes. The committee knows KBTA well (Yuval's framework), so stay brief. Knowledge-based temporal abstraction converts time-stamped clinical data into interval-level, clinically named concepts: a set of (concept, start, end). A run of glucose measurements becomes, for example, GLUCOSE:HIGH holding for eleven hours, then GLUCOSE:DECREASING for four. Abstraction types: raw concepts; states (knowledge-defined thresholds); trends (what counts as a meaningful change over what span); events (medication, procedure, diagnosed episode); contexts (an interpretation frame such as a diabetes diagnosis, which changes how other abstractions are derived, for example shorter relevance for glucose because it is re-measured sooner); and patterns, composite concepts over other abstractions that open at an anchor and close at an action. Two knowledge parameters: thresholds (context-sensitive) and relevance durations (persistence), which let two measurements join into one interval or leave a gap where the record is silent. The key distinction for later: unlike equal-width, equal-frequency or SAX-like σ-bin discretization, KBTA boundaries are clinical and named. The σ-bin arm in the ablations is exactly that knowledge-free alternative.
