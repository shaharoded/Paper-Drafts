Level and trend of one enzyme get opposite signs

Ben-Gurion University of the Negev · Shahar Oded

23

About 1.5 minutes. Per-token attributions for death, aggregated over 300 test patients through the gradient-decoupled sparse transcoders. Red pushes predicted risk up, blue down. Positive side: dominated by severe hypoalbuminemia, a well-established mortality marker, followed by elevated aminotransferase (liver stress) and rising bicarbonate (acid–base decompensation). The negative side is the more informative one: recovery-associated concepts, a decreasing trend in AST and a subcutaneous basal-insulin treatment state. The model separates a decreasing enzyme trend from an elevated enzyme level and gives them opposite signs, so the attribution tracks clinical direction, not just clinical activity. A method keyed to how often a concept appears could not do that. Every contribution is a named clinical concept, and clinically coherent patterns appear across all six outcomes (Appendix figure). Caveat: local sensitivity of the fitted model, not causal necessity, and not a statement about the patient.
