About 2 minutes. This is the second kind of knowledge: procedural quality-assessment knowledge. A recommendation is formalized as a quality-assessment temporal pattern (QATP): an anchor identifying the situation where it applies, an action that satisfies it, a constraint type, and a membership function grading partial compliance. Three constraint types, which are also the three compliance aspects we report: cyclic (an action expected to recur, counted per window, e.g. routine glucose monitoring), time (a required gap between anchor and action, e.g. insulin within hours of hyperglycemia), and value (a required range, usually a dose). Temporal fuzzy logic: practice rarely splits cleanly into compliant and non-compliant. In the example, insulin is expected within 4 hours and considered missed after 12. Insulin after 8 hours scores 0.5 rather than an outright failure. All three constraint types are scored with trapezoids like this. From instances to a QA profile: the Mediator emits one scored instance per triggering occasion. Aggregation is staged averaging: per day and aspect, then across aspects, then across days, then equal-weight across recommendations. So frequently triggered requirements cannot dominate, and the profile stays decomposable down to the recommendation and day. Conventions worth knowing if asked: a recommendation that never became applicable produces no score (not a zero); where an action must be withheld, no response counts as compliant.
