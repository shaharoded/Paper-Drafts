About 1 minute. This creates a twofold need. Computationally, we need models that reason over the irregular, sparse, heterogeneous temporal data of an EHR well enough to anticipate short-term deterioration, while staying interpretable enough for clinicians to act on. Clinically, we need to measure how well guidelines are actually followed across hospitals and over time, and whether adherence translates into better outcomes. The thesis treats them as one problem. Two kinds of medical knowledge are involved. Declarative knowledge (what is "persistent hyperglycemia") turns raw measurements into named concepts. Procedural quality-control knowledge (did the clinician give insulin after a hyperglycemic episode) describes the treatment process. Most predictive EHR work uses the first and ignores the second. We argue the second carries independent signal, and it is the part clinicians can actually change.
