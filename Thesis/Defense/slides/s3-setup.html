About 1.5 minutes. Setup: training draws one sample per admission per context window over K = 1 to 13 days, to make the model robust to how much history it gets; all inference is at K = 4 days with a label window of (96, 336] hours. 179,141 training samples, 4,600 validation and 4,686 test admissions, split by patient. At K = 4 only eight of the ten complications clear the 1% prevalence threshold; hyperosmolality and ketoacidosis do not. The ladder, from raw measurements through knowledge-free intervals to knowledge-based abstractions, each with and without the admission's QA profile: - Arms 1 to 4 never see the Mediator's intervals. When paired with QA (arms 2 and 4), the compliance signal enters only as an aggregated static vector appended to their features. - Arm 5: INTERVenE-Enc on σ-bin intervals, same raw data, no clinical knowledge. - Arm 6: INTERVenE-Enc on knowledge-based intervals. - Arm 7: KB plus QA, where compliance patterns appear as temporal tokens the encoder's attention can align with the interval stream (and the profile also through the context pathway). Contrasts: arm 6 vs arm 3 is the Kineret analogue of MIMIC's raw-vs-abstracted comparison; arm 6 vs arm 5 isolates the knowledge base itself (same architecture, same data). Each arm run once at the deployed MIMIC-IV configuration, no tuning: compute in the regulated environment is limited and the question is transferability, not best achievable performance.
