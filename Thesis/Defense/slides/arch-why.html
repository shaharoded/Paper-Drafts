- Each row: what the component does, the experiment that tested it, and what happened - START + END: each interval becomes two tokens at its boundaries, so duration is the time between them and overlapping states stay visible. Experiment: same data, each interval reduced to one token at its start. Duration and overlap are gone: AUPRC −0.079, LoS error +7.4 h. Strongest evidence on the slide - Hierarchical embedding: a token is four embeddings concatenated (raw concept → abstraction type → concept with value → START/END role). GLUCOSE_STATE and GLUCOSE_TREND share GLUCOSE; an interval's two ends share three of four. Evidence: after training, embedding distances still follow the hierarchy exactly, so the structure is kept, not washed out (the hypothesis from slide 13) - Time2Vec: hours since admission encoded as one linear + several learned periodic terms, concatenated to the concept embeddings and projected, instead of added. Adding forces identity and time to share one subspace. Not ablated - Temporal RoPE: the rotation inside attention is driven by elapsed hours, not token index. Two tokens attend by how many hours apart they are, not how many events were recorded between them, which varies by patient and ward. Without it the decoder generates trajectories of the wrong length (1.8× the true length) and AUPRC drops 0.037 - AdaLN-Zero: static context (age, sex, admission info; in Study 3 also the QA profile) sets the scale and shift of every block's normalization, initialized to zero so it starts as a no-op; context dropout stops over-reliance. Not ablated; its payoff is that QA enters with no architecture change - Attention pool, what it does: one learned query vector per outcome. Each query attends over the final hidden states of the whole admission and returns that outcome's own weighted summary, so DEATH and HYPOGLYCEMIA can read different tokens. The summary goes to a shared MLP, then a risk logit and a time estimate - Attention pool, the experiment: in the decoder it was added in phase 3 as an auxiliary admission-level head next to the step-wise risk head (10k search): AUPRC 0.542 → 0.562, the largest single gain. The encoder then uses it as its main readout - Capacity: inverted U; 14× the parameters buy nothing, and even the 0.46M model beats ss-STraTS, so the gain is the architecture, not size
