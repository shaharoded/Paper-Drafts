Firth regression per outcome and cutoff; robust = significant with the same sign at 24, 48 and 72 h

Ben-Gurion University of the Negev · Shahar Oded

38

- Question: which individual recommendations are associated with each complication? One model per complication, at cutoffs of 24, 48 and 72 h; adherence and covariates measured only up to the cutoff.

What Firth is: logistic regression with a small penalty on the likelihood (Jeffreys prior) that pulls estimates away from infinity and removes small-sample bias. Same odds ratios to read, just stable.

Why we needed it: separation. When a predictor (almost) perfectly splits events from non-events, which happens with rare outcomes and many components, plain maximum likelihood pushes that coefficient to infinity and never converges. That happened for most complications; Firth always gives finite estimates - Row design: positive if the complication first occurs at or after the cutoff, negative if never; admissions with the event before the cutoff are dropped (care cannot cause a pre-existing condition), so event counts fall as the cutoff moves - Correlated predictors pruned, rest standardized. 

Robust = significant with the same sign at all three cutoffs; glucose monitoring left out of glycemic models (it is how those events are detected) - Results: 9 to 34 predictors retained per outcome, 0 to 32 robust, tracking event counts. Mortality (3,130 events) gives 32 robust; six of ten outcomes give none, so the criterion filters noise rather than inventing findings - Face validity: the mortality model recovers age, RDW, BMI, dementia and cardiovascular comorbidity unprompted, and several adherence components rank among its strongest predictors - Across outcomes, insulin after hyperglycemia and admission glucose monitoring recur most; signs can differ between endpoints - These are associations, not effects; the next slides test them with IPTW
