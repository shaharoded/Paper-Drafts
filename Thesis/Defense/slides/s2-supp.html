- Quick slide; only if time allows - Adherence does not depend on documentation: 0.669 vs 0.661 (difference 0.008) among admissions with hyperglycemia in the first 48 h; significant only because of the sample size, not clinically meaningful. Undocumented patients are harder to find, not treated differently, so a measurement-based cohort definition is safe - OR 0.757: odds ratio for 30-day death per one-standard-deviation increase in whole-admission adherence, so about 24% lower odds. Logistic regression adjusted for age, sex, modified Charlson index and hospital, SEs clustered on patient. Unadjusted: 16.8% lower odds per 0.1-point increase (OR 0.832), so the relationship is graded, not a threshold. Descriptive only: whole-admission adherence does not strictly precede death - Daily trajectories (supplementary): is an admission's adherence score steady day to day, or pulled down by a few bad days? Among 226,568 admission–recommendation pairs with at least 3 scored days, 36.1% were nearly flat (worst day at least 90% of the mean) and 32.9% had a worst day below 60% of the mean. So about one third of averages hide one or more low-scoring days. Both types at every hospital; for insulin after hyperglycemia the share with a bad day ranged 30% to 60% across hospitals. Length of stay barely related (Spearman ρ = −0.110 with mean, −0.064 with variability). Motivates a per-day profile (future work)
