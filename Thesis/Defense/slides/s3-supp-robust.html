- Calibration asks a different question from AUPRC: not "does the model rank sick patients higher" but "when it says 20% risk, do about 20% of those patients have the event?" That matters if clinicians read the number itself - ECE (expected calibration error): bin predictions into 15 probability bins, compare mean predicted risk with the observed event rate in each bin, average the gaps. KB 0.012 vs ss-STraTS 0.127: KB's stated risks are off by about 1 point on average, ss-STraTS by about 13 - Brier score: mean squared error between predicted probability and the 0/1 outcome; it mixes ranking and calibration, lower is better. KB 0.066 vs ss-STraTS 0.110 - As emitted = the raw output probabilities, before any temperature scaling. KB comes out calibrated without a fix; scaling on validation only takes its ECE from 0.012 to 0.010. Averaged over the 8 outcomes with equal weight - Caveat: descriptive, no paired test, and says nothing about benefit at a specific clinical decision threshold - Width sensitivity answers "is it just more parameters?": no - Mediator labels are stable under small measurement changes (500 people, 26 numeric states) - Density comparison is descriptive; it cannot attribute performance to recording frequency
