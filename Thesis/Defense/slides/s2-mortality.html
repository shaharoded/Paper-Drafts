Study 2 · Influence

More adherence, fewer deaths

Each +0.1 in adherence: 17% lower odds of 30-day death

Ben-Gurion University of the Negev · Shahar Oded

30

About 1.5 minutes. Survivors had higher mean adherence than those who died (0.693 vs 0.652, P < .001). Mortality fell across adherence strata from about 11% to about 3% (strata under 30 admissions suppressed; strata are for display, not the basis of the test). On the continuous score, each 0.1-point increase was associated with 16.8% lower odds of death (OR 0.832, 95% CI 0.811–0.853), so the association is graded rather than threshold-like. Adjusting for age, sex, comorbidity burden and hospital left it intact (adjusted OR 0.757, 95% CI 0.727–0.788, per 1-SD increase). Important caveat, say it before someone asks: this whole-admission analysis is descriptive. Whole-admission adherence spans the entire stay, including the period right before death, when guideline-concordant care is often deliberately de-escalated. So exposure does not strictly precede outcome here. The component-level and propensity-weighted analyses on the next slides fix that by scoring adherence over a fixed early window. Also: adherence to the insulin recommendations showed negligible correlation with hypoglycemic burden (r = 0.052; severe r = 0.056; n = 40,719).
