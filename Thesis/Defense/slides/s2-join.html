About 1 minute. Slow down here; this is the clinical punchline. The components with the largest implementation gaps are the same ones most strongly associated with outcomes. Insulin after hyperglycemia (mean adherence 0.15) and routine basal insulin dosing (0.21 to 0.23) are both the least-implemented recommendations assessed and, after weighting, among those most strongly associated with lower mortality and with less severe hyperglycemia. Both are recommendations that call for repeated decisions distributed across a stay, rather than one discrete action at one decision point. That makes them natural targets for decision support: a prompt at the point of care can plausibly help with exactly this kind of task, and unlike the admission recommendations they are nowhere near ceiling. A reminder to order creatinine would change almost nothing, because creatinine is already ordered. Keep the language associational: these are balance-verified associations, not causal effects.
