About 1.5 minutes. Keep this short. Diabetes is a global epidemic. In Israel, people with diabetes are more than 7% of the population but account for roughly one third of total health-care spending (Israeli national diabetes registry). They take a disproportionate share of inpatient days and drive costly complications: infection, acute kidney injury, cardiovascular events, ketoacidosis. Inside the hospital, dysglycemia is not a static comorbidity, it is an actively managed process. Hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia during admission prolong length of stay and increase mortality, and both depend largely on how insulin is titrated day by day. Adherence to evidence-based insulin protocols is inconsistent in routine internal-medicine practice. Israel recently intervened: an updated national position paper (September 2023), a mandatory staff e-learning module, and a new quality indicator for persistent hyperglycemia. Their real-world effect had not been quantified at scale.
