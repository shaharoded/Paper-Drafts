About 1.5 minutes. 1. A transformer architecture over temporal abstraction intervals. INTERVenE takes symbolic intervals as native input: each interval becomes a START and END token embedded through a four-level concept hierarchy, elapsed time through Time2Vec and temporal rotary embeddings, static context through AdaLN-Zero. Two variants developed and compared. The encoder outperforms the established neural and symbolic alternatives on a shared benchmark and leads every comparator on both cohorts. 2. Evidence about what interval representations contribute. Holding the architecture fixed and varying only the input separates two effects: collapsing intervals to points degrades performance sharply, replacing named intervals with σ-bins does not (on MIMIC-IV). The gain belongs to persistence and concurrency, which bounds what a knowledge base buys a predictor and makes the architecture applicable to interval data outside medicine. The Kineret repetition qualifies it: there the substitution did cost accuracy. 3. Attribution that resolves to named concepts. Sparse transcoders through a gradient-decoupled hook whose injected term is numerically zero: bit-exact forward pass, the deployed model's own logits explained. 4. A released software stack: a reimplementation of the Temporal Mediator doing abstraction and adherence scoring in one pass over a TAK repository; a KarmaLego mining pipeline with TPF; INTERVenE with its attribution pipeline. All pure Python, deployable in ordinary environments, including the regulated hospital platform this work ran inside.
