Inpatient hyperglycemia is common, harmful, and governed by clinical guide-
lines whose adherence is rarely measured. This thesis addresses two con-
nected problems: whether guideline adherence can be assessed automatically
and at scale, and whether a model reasoning over clinically meaningful tem-
poral abstractions can predict the complications those guidelines exist to
prevent.
iii
Both rest on a single representation. A diabetes knowledge base, authored
for this work, encodes the clinical semantics of inpatient care and the rec-
ommendations of the Israeli national position paper as quality-assessment
temporal patterns. One abstraction engine converts an OMOP-formatted
record into knowledge-based temporal abstractions and, in the same pass,
scores how far the recorded care departed from what the guideline required.
The methodological study introducesINTERVenE, a transformer family
operating natively on those intervals, with an autoregressive variant that gen-
erates abstraction trajectories with per-step risk curves and a bidirectional
variant for single-pass prediction. On 57,078 MIMIC-IV diabetic admissions,
the encoder reaches a support-weighted AUPRC of 0.672, improving on the
strongest neural baseline by 0.041 with non-overlapping confidence intervals.
Sparse-transcoder attribution, computed on the deployed model through a
gradient-decoupled hook, resolves every explanation to a named clinical con-
cept. Ablations show that the architecture depends on the persistence of
intervals rather than on their clinical naming: knowledge-based abstraction
is an interpretability substrate obtained at essentially no cost in accuracy,
not a predictive prerequisite.
The clinical study applies the same machinery to 48,354 admissions across six
Israeli general hospitals. Adherence divides not by clinical importance but
by the kind of action required: discrete admission actions are near-universal,
while recommendations calling for repeated insulin decisions across a stay
score as low as 0.15, most often absent entirely. Adherence rose modestly
after national implementation, with marked inter-hospital heterogeneity. Un-

der propensity weighting, above-median adherence carried 44% lower odds
of 30-day mortality and 82% lower odds of hyperglycemia, against a 20%
increase in hypoglycemia — a lopsided trade-off, and the one these recom-

proved to be among those most strongly associated with outcomes.
Finally, the two halves are joined: the transformer is trained on the clinical
cohort with and without the measured adherence profile as input, testing
whether a quality-of-care signal carries predictive information beyond the
record it was derived from.
