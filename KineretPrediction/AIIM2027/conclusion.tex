\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
In adults hospitalized with diabetes or hyperglycemia, clinically defined intervals gave INTERVenE-Enc better discrimination than statistically defined intervals, and the interval based encoder outperformed models of using raw measurements and observations. Together with the ICU findings, this supports the approach in two health systems with different recording practices and with models fitted in each setting. In Kineret, guideline assessments improved discrimination for hyperglycemia, but reduced it for mortality, and offered no clear overall gain. For every outcome, however, the model's sensitivity could be traced to named patterns of care as well as to the patient's physiological history unlike models where the input tokens are not mapped to meaningful concepts.
