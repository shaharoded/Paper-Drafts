\section{Data Preparation, Knowledge Acquisition, and Software}
\label{app:data-prep}

\paragraph{Source and unified tables.}
The extraction consumed the OMOP tables \texttt{person},
\texttt{visit\_occurrence}, \texttt{visit\_detail}, \texttt{death},
\texttt{condition\_occurrence}, \texttt{observation},
\texttt{procedure\_occurrence}, \texttt{device\_exposure},
\texttt{drug\_exposure}, and \texttt{measurement}, and reorganized them into
five unified tables: \emph{visits}, \emph{visit types}, \emph{clinical
events} (conditions, observations, procedures, and device exposures under a
common schema), \emph{drug exposures}, and \emph{measurements}. The only
enrichments applied were the attachment of Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical
codes to drug exposures, where available, the harmonization of column types,
and the closing of intervals whose end timestamp was missing or not later
than their start one second after their start, so that point observations
stored under an interval schema carry an explicit rather than a degenerate
duration. Dimension tables enumerating the distinct clinical, measurement,
and drug concepts, together with their units and administration routes, were
derived from the unified tables and used to support concept curation.

\paragraph{Concept curation.}
Candidate codes for each target concept were surfaced by descriptive
analytics over the dimension tables, ranked by frequency of occurrence and by
apparent relevance to the concept, and then accepted or rejected by the
clinical domain expert. The resulting indices define both the concepts
included in the abstraction input and the departmental and diagnostic
exclusions applied in the cohort definition. Exclusions were applied through
these curated indices rather than inline rules, so that each exclusion is
recorded once alongside the concept vocabulary and applied uniformly across
the pipeline. The curated indices, rather than the exploratory analytics used
to generate the candidate lists, are the reproducible artifact of this step,
and are provided with this supplement.

\paragraph{Derived concepts.}
Body mass index was computed from paired height and weight measurements where
not recorded directly. Ketoacidosis events were derived from combinations of
pH, bicarbonate, and intravenous insulin administration, as described in
Appendix~\ref{app:definitions}.

\paragraph{Modified Charlson comorbidity index.}
The score is a weighted sum of ten chronic condition flags ascertained from
the first 48 hours of admission: malignancy, chronic kidney disease, and
chronic liver disease weighted 2; and cardiac disease, chronic respiratory
disease, diabetes, dementia, alcohol misuse, anemia, and recurrent falls
weighted 1. One additional point is added per decade of age above 50.
Categories absent from the original Charlson instrument reflect the condition
groupings that could be resolved from the unmapped source vocabulary during
curation.

\paragraph{Threshold acquisition.}
Discretization thresholds, relevance durations, and trend limits were
acquired in two passes. One-dimensional $k$-means and kernel density
estimation over each concept's value distribution proposed candidate
boundaries; these were then reconciled against published reference ranges and
the definitions in the national guideline, which were authoritative, with the
data-driven candidates used only to resolve cases where the literature gives
a range rather than a value. Concepts with too many distinct value
combinations for in-engine discretization, such as corticosteroid
preparations at varying doses, were pre-discretized during data preparation.

\paragraph{Aggregation of adherence scores.}
The admission is the unit of analysis throughout. A patient may contribute
several admissions, and patient-level grouping is applied wherever the
statistics require it, as in the clustered standard errors of the mortality
model. Compliance scores were aggregated in stages designed so that neither
frequently-evaluated patterns nor frequently-evaluated days dominate the
result. Scores were first averaged within each admission, pattern, compliance
aspect, and calendar day; aspects were then collapsed within each admission,
pattern, and day; days were then collapsed within each admission and pattern;
and finally patterns were averaged with equal weight to give one overall
adherence score per admission. Component-level analyses use the corresponding
pattern-level and aspect-level aggregates.

\paragraph{Software.}
All processing and analysis were performed in Python 3.13. The extraction and
cohort-construction pipeline used \texttt{dask} and \texttt{pyarrow} over
Parquet storage, with \texttt{pandas} and \texttt{numpy} for in-memory
operations; empirical threshold discovery used $k$-means clustering and
kernel density estimation from \texttt{scikit-learn} with peak detection from
\texttt{scipy}. Guideline adherence was computed using the Mediator
abstraction engine \citep{mediator-github}, which validates the knowledge
base against its XML schema before evaluating the abstractions. Statistical
analysis used \texttt{statsmodels} for the logistic, propensity-weighted, and
Welch comparisons, together with its Benjamini--Hochberg implementation for
multiplicity correction. Bias-reduced logistic regression was implemented
directly, following Firth's iteratively reweighted least-squares formulation
with a Jeffreys-prior penalty and standard errors taken from the penalized
information matrix; it is released with the analysis code rather than drawn
from a library.

\section{Operational Definitions of Complications}
\label{app:definitions}

Definitions are given in the order used in the main-text tables. The glycemic
endpoints and acute kidney injury are derived within the knowledge base from
the corresponding measurements, while ketoacidosis, infection,
hyperosmolality and myocardial infarction were computed during data
preparation and supplied to the engine as primitive concepts.

\paragraph{Thirty-day mortality.}
Death recorded in the linked mortality data between 14 days before and 30
days after the recorded discharge datetime. The backward tolerance ensures
that in-hospital deaths are captured where the death record precedes the
administrative discharge timestamp.

\paragraph{Hyperglycemia.}
Either a single glucose measurement $\geq$250 mg/dL, or a sustained
hyperglycemic state, defined as a glucose measurement $\geq$180 mg/dL whose
immediately preceding measurement within the previous 24 hours was also
$\geq$180 mg/dL. The sustained-state requirement prevents an isolated
elevated reading from being counted as a complication.

\paragraph{Severe hyperglycemia.}
Any single glucose measurement $\geq$250 mg/dL. This endpoint overlaps by
construction with hyperglycemia and is retained as a separate, more specific
target.

\paragraph{Hypoglycemia.}
Either a single glucose measurement $\leq$54 mg/dL, or a sustained
hypoglycemic state, defined as a glucose measurement $\leq$70 mg/dL whose
immediately preceding measurement within the previous 24 hours was also
$\leq$70 mg/dL.

\paragraph{Severe hypoglycemia.}
Any single glucose measurement $\leq$54 mg/dL.

\paragraph{Inpatient acute kidney injury.}
Laboratory-defined acute kidney injury of stage 2 or above, established
either by a serum creatinine ratio $\geq$2.0 relative to the patient's
baseline, or by an absolute serum creatinine $\geq$4.0 mg/dL. Stage~1 injury
was deliberately excluded from the endpoint.

\paragraph{Inpatient myocardial infarction.}
Evidence of an ischemic cardiac event---ischemic heart disease, myocardial
infarction, coronary intervention, cardiac catheterization, or
revascularization---together with a troponin measurement of at least 600. The
troponin requirement excludes coded cardiac history unaccompanied by
biochemical evidence of acute injury.

\paragraph{Hyperosmolality.}
Emitted by either of two paths. The derived path implements a
hyperosmolar-hyperglycemic-state rule: for each glucose measurement
$\geq$600 mg/dL, the nearest serum sodium measurement within $\pm$6 hours is
located and the effective osmolality computed as
$2 \times \mathrm{Na} + \mathrm{glucose}/18$; an event is emitted where this
exceeds 320 mOsm/kg, provided no acidosis event occurs within $\pm$6 hours of
the triggering glucose draw. The acidosis exclusion separates hyperosmolar
states from diabetic ketoacidosis. The observation path additionally emits an
event wherever hyperosmolality is directly coded.

\paragraph{Ketoacidosis.}
Emitted by either of two paths. The observation path emits an event wherever
ketoacidosis is directly coded. The derived path requires the conjunction of
a pH measurement $\leq$7.3, a bicarbonate measurement $\leq$10 mEq/L within
24 hours of it, and intravenous insulin administration within 6 hours of
it---that is, biochemical acidosis together with the treatment response it
implies.

\paragraph{Infection.}
Requires a physiological trigger, corroborating diagnostic evidence, and
treatment, all within a single episode. The trigger is any one of: fever
onset, defined as at least two body-temperature measurements
$\geq$37.8$^\circ$C where all temperatures in the preceding 48 hours were
$\leq$37.7$^\circ$C; leukocytosis, defined as a white-cell count $\geq$11
where counts in the preceding 48 hours lay between 4 and 11; leukopenia,
defined as a white-cell count $\leq$4 under the same prior-stability
condition; or neutropenia, defined as a neutrophil count $\leq$1.5 where
preceding counts within 48 hours exceeded 1.5. Diagnostic evidence requires
at least one blood culture, urine culture, or chest radiograph within
$\pm$24 hours of the trigger, the latest such record within the window
serving as the episode anchor. Treatment requires at least one antibiotic
administration after that anchor and within 72 hours of the trigger. Triggers
occurring within 72 hours of an already-accepted event are collapsed into it,
so an admission may contribute multiple distinct episodes. The
prior-stability condition attached to every trigger restricts the endpoint to
infections arising during the hospitalization rather than present on
admission.

\section{Assessed Guideline Recommendations}
\label{app:patterns}

Fourteen quality-assessment patterns were evaluated, corresponding to the
components reported in the main text (Table~\ref{tab:pattern-definitions}).
Each pattern defines an applicability condition, so that it is scored only
for admissions and time points at which the underlying recommendation
applies, and one or more compliance aspects. Three aspect types are used:
\emph{time}, whether a required action occurred within a required interval;
\emph{cyclic}, whether a recurring action occurred at the required frequency;
and \emph{value}, whether an administered dose met the required magnitude.
Insulin administration after hyperglycemia is a limiting case of the time
aspect: it is defined by an overlap relation rather than by a delay, so no
distance separates anchor from action for a membership function to grade, and
the constraint is satisfied on existence alone---scoring 1 where the action is
present and 0 where it is not.

Each aspect is scored by a trapezoidal membership function, so the reported
values are continuous on $[0,1]$: full credit is given throughout the
interval in which the recommendation is considered met, with linear partial
credit on either side of it and zero beyond the outer bounds. Several
trapezoids are deliberately open on one side---full credit at or above a
required count, for instance, with no penalty for exceeding it---so that
partial credit applies only where departing from the recommendation in that
direction is meaningful. A recommendation
carrying more than one aspect is reported under each aspect separately, which
is why routine basal dosage appears twice below---once for the frequency of
administration and once for the dose administered. The knowledge base
additionally contains a metformin continuation pattern; it is defined for
detection only and carries no compliance function, so it yields no score and
is not among the fourteen assessed components.

The differing treatment of unanswered anchors in the two dose-reduction
patterns is deliberate and reflects how the two insulins are used. Basal
insulin is given routinely, so a hypoglycemic event with no basal dose in the
following 24 hours represents the dose having been withheld, which satisfies
the recommendation and scores 1. Bolus insulin is given reactively, so the
absence of a dose carries no information about whether a reduction was
warranted, and those events are excluded from scoring rather than counted as
compliant. This is the principal reason the two components report such
different means (0.87 and 0.28) despite sharing a membership function.

\section{Daily Trajectories of Adherence Scores}

We examined whether admission-level adherence scores reflected consistently
similar daily performance or were driven by a small number of low-scoring days.
Among admission--pattern pairs with at least three scored days (226,568 pairs),
36.1\% had a minimum daily score at least 90\% of their mean, whereas 32.9\%
had a minimum below 60\% of their mean, indicating substantial heterogeneity
in within-admission stability. Both trajectory types occurred at every
hospital, although their frequency varied; for insulin after hyperglycemia,
the share with a low-scoring day ranged from 30\% to 60\% across hospitals.
Length of stay had only weak inverse correlations with mean daily adherence
(Spearman $\rho=-0.110$) and within-admission variability
($\rho=-0.064$); the latter suggests little relationship between stay length
and score variability despite its small $P$ value in this large sample. Daily
trajectories were therefore mixed: roughly one-third were nearly flat, while a
similar share of admission--pattern averages concealed one or more low-scoring
days.
